01ABOUT ME

A little
about me.

I've always liked building things. Even before software became my thing, I enjoyed coming up with ideas, making little projects, and seeing if I could turn them into something real.

That hasn't really changed. I still like starting with an idea, figuring things out as I go, and seeing where it takes me. Sometimes it's a side project, sometimes an experiment, and sometimes just something I wanted to try.

I enjoy the problem-solving side of building — figuring out what actually needs to be built, making trade-offs, and finding a simple way forward. Side projects and open source give me a way to keep doing that outside of work.

Outside of tech, I like staying active, travelling, trekking, playing sports, and occasionally getting lost in a good documentary or movie.

01

News Platform

I started with a simple question: could I build a better way to discover and read news? What followed was several months of research, feasibility work, and navigating the practical and compliance challenges of running an RSS-based news platform. I built the ingestion and processing pipeline, worked on article clustering, deduplication, semantic search, and personalization, and eventually realized the bigger opportunity was somewhere else.

The project ultimately pivoted into a daily hyperlocal newsletter.

Engineering highlight

Built an end-to-end news pipeline for ingestion, deduplication, semantic clustering, and personalized delivery.

02

JARVIS

I'm a Marvel fan, so naturally I wanted my own JARVIS. What started as a fun experiment became a local AI assistant for things I actually wanted help with — searching the web, managing reminders and notes, logging work, maintaining context, and experimenting with agentic workflows.

It's still a project I come back to whenever I want to explore an idea or try a new approach to AI.

03

AI Summarizer

When Chrome introduced built-in AI capabilities, I wanted to see what could be built with them. Rather than making another tool that simply summarizes a page, I explored the whole journey: read → understand → test what you learned.

That led to a Chrome extension that can summarize pages, answer questions, and generate quizzes, with the AI running on-device.

Engineering highlight

Used browser-native AI to keep page summaries, Q&A, and quizzes running privately on-device.

React UI Templates

This one came from a much more practical problem. While freelancing, I found myself repeatedly building the same CRUD screens and UI patterns. Instead of continuing to copy and paste, I built a reusable UI system driven by JSON configuration.

It reduced repetitive implementation, helped me deliver more within the same constraints, and left more time for the parts that actually needed engineering.
